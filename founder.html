As you can see, we make some money there. That's Upwork alone — LinkedIn and referrals bring in more. I'm showing it because it's the part you can go and check yourself.

But here's what I know and feel every week:

  • You pay to apply, not for a lead. The platform takes 10%. Applying costs 15–30 connects, so $2–5 — except we all know plain applying is useless, so you add a boost, another ~50 connects, another ~$7.50. That's around $10 to apply. Not for a lead. Not for a contact. For a shot at being seen by a posting that might be fake anyway. Then visibility badges, profile boost, subscription, withdrawal fee.
  • Most leads never answer. Even the ones who reply first.
  • Client-side rating pressure. On fixed price you're not really negotiating from a strong position, and everyone knows it.

And still — Upwork might be the best place to start a digital agency, because the leads are always there. It's not good, it's not bad, it is what it is. Getting work as an agency from Upwork is far easier than from LinkedIn with no established brand, no marketing and sales budget, and no ability to survive long sales cycles. I'm not even talking about AI yet.

So don't get me wrong. I'm not against Upwork. We use it and we'll keep using it. Upwork is like your ex — you know them, you know what to expect, zero enjoyment.

So we found another source of leads.

What it actually does

  1. You set a keyword — React, Webflow, whatever you sell. New postings that match land in your Telegram or Slack, usually about two minutes after they go up.
  2. Each one arrives with the name attached: the person or company behind the posting, and the profile to message.
  3. You pitch them directly. No connects, no proposal queue, no cut on what you win.

There's also a searchable archive of everything already posted, going back — useful when you're thinking about a new service line and want to see what the market actually paid for it before you hire anyone. Easier to show on a call than to describe here.
